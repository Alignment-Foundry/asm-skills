# Pricing Strategy — Service Business

User preference: **Value-based premium positioning, not commodity pricing.**

## Core Principle

When asked to evaluate or propose pricing for the user's service business:

1. **Lead with the value, not the floor.** The user's service is premium — structure pricing to reflect that. Cheap introductory tiers signal commodity, not confidence.
2. **Use discounts as a relationship tool, not a default.** Initial/founding clients get a 3-month free pilot + discounted first year (30% off). This is presented as a **founding client benefit**, not a reflection of the service's actual value.
3. **Standard pricing IS the premium price.** The $1,200-1,800/mo range (for the agency) is the real price of the service, not a "premium add-on" above cheap tiers. Don't suggest lowering the standard price to attract clients.
4. **Negotiation room comes from the discount, not from showing a lower standard price.** The 30% off year one gives ~$360-540/mo of room to flex per client, while the published price stays premium.

## How This Affects Recommendations

- **When presented with cheap tiers on a pricing page:** Don't validate them as "good for getting started" — flag that they undervalue the service and propose raising them to the premium level.
- **When structuring initial client offers:** Frame it as: "Standard price is $X. As a founding client, you get 3 months free + year one at 30% off. Full price from year two." Never frame it as "we're testing the market at a lower rate."
- **When asked if pricing is reasonable:** The question isn't "is $1,200 too high for the market?" — it's "does the service deliver $1,200 of value?" Assume yes unless proven otherwise.

## Price Anchoring Example

| Wrong approach | Right approach |
|---|---|
| "Starter at $199 is a low barrier to entry while you prove the model" | "$199 signals a commodity service — the real value is $1,200/mo. Lead with that." |
| "The $1,200-1,800 range works for founding clients who get premium attention" | "$1,200-1,800 is the standard price. Founding clients get a discount as a relationship gesture." |
